SB 684 / SB 1123 Site Criteria

What Makes an
SB 684 Site Work.

Infill lots in the flats, usually with a dated house on them, big enough for six or more new fee-simple homes. If a property meets most of this list, send it: we’ll run the full eligibility review.

Read the SB 684 guide →

Infill, in the flats

Westside first: Culver City, Santa Monica, Mar Vista, Palms, Del Rey, West LA, Westchester. Select coastal markets with strong per-square-foot resale.

5,000 SF minimum

6,000 SF or more preferred. Room for at least six homes, each with one parking space.

Corner or street-to-alley

Two frontages make a better site plan. Mid-block lots still considered.

Multifamily zoning preferred

Single-family lots qualify under SB 1123 only if vacant, which can include a lot whose only structure is abandoned and uninhabitable.

No renters in 5 years

Owner-occupied, or no rental history in the last five years.

No Ellis Act in 15 years

No Ellis Act withdrawal on the property in the last fifteen years.

Outside fire hazard zones

Not in a high or very high fire hazard severity zone, which rules out most hillside lots.

Clear of other exclusions

No fault zones, flood hazard areas, wetlands, protected habitat, hazardous waste sites or conservation easements.

Not already split

No prior subdivision under SB 9 or SB 684.

A screening summary, not legal advice. Final eligibility depends on the local agency’s review of the specific site.

Luxury Residential Criteria

What Makes a
Luxury Deal Work.

Our first choice is an under-improved house in a strong neighborhood that we can remodel, expand, finish to a high standard and sell in a short time frame. Teardowns are considered, underwritten more conservatively.

Class A, A− and B+ only

Westside preferred; hillside projects only in select cases.

Typically $1M–$3M

We pay more in stronger neighborhoods where resale supports it.

Remodel first

Keeping part of the walls or foundation lets the work be permitted and financed as a remodel, which takes far less capital.

Room to add square footage

Unused floor area or an expandable footprint, so the work adds real value.

Teardowns, selectively

Ground-up luxury sites, with more conservative margins for the added cost and time.

Hillside: views and flat pads

We pass on steep lots needing heavy retaining or caissons, sites without privacy, and homes of three or more levels.

Below Measure ULA in LA

In the City of LA, we generally buy so the finished home sells under $5,400,000, unless the margin clearly justifies it.

Higher prices outside LA

Beverly Hills proper and select Orange County and South Bay markets, where ULA doesn’t apply.

No design review

We pass on design review boards, historic overlay zones (HPOZ) and the Coastal Zone.

Measure ULA threshold per the City of Los Angeles Office of Finance, effective July 1, 2026. It applies to sales within City of Los Angeles limits.

Questions

For Agents & Wholesalers.

Do you work with buyer’s and listing agents?

Yes. We work with agents on both sides and honor the agreed commission. Bring us a deal and you stay on it.

Do you buy on assignment from wholesalers?

Yes. Send deals you have under contract, with the contract price and your assignment terms, and we’ll tell you quickly whether it works for us.

How fast can you close?

When the deal calls for it, we can close quickly with cash or private financing. Timing depends on title, the seller’s needs and any due diligence the site requires; we’ll tell you our timeline with the offer.

What happens after I submit?

Within two business days you’ll get a yes, a no, or our questions. If it fits, we walk the property and make an offer.

Do you pay referral fees?

Yes. If you’re not an agent and bring us an off-market property we buy, we pay a referral fee where the law allows it. Tell us up front when you submit, and we’ll confirm terms in writing before you share the owner’s details.

Do you look at deals outside these neighborhoods?

Yes, selectively. For luxury single-family projects we also buy in Beverly Hills proper and select Orange County and South Bay markets, where Measure ULA doesn’t apply. For SB 684 and SB 1123, we also consider coastal and infill sites in the South Bay, Orange County, San Diego and Santa Barbara where new homes can achieve strong per-square-foot resale. If you’re unsure, send it.
