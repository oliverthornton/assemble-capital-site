SB 684 / SB 1123 Site Criteria

What Makes an
SB 684 Site Work.

These are infill lots in the flats, usually with a dated single-family home on them, big enough for at least six new fee-simple homes. We prefer multifamily-zoned parcels on the Westside, in Class A, A− and B+ neighborhoods, and every site has to clear the tenant-history and hazard rules in the law. If a property meets most of this list, send it: we’ll run the full eligibility review.

Read the full SB 684 / 1123 guide
  • Location: infill lots in the flats. Westside markets first, in Class A / A− / B+ neighborhoods: Culver City, Santa Monica, Mar Vista, Palms, Del Rey, West Los Angeles, Westchester, Marina del Rey, Venice, Brentwood and similar. We also consider coastal and infill sites in the South Bay, Orange County, San Diego and Santa Barbara where the new homes can conservatively achieve strong per-square-foot resale.
  • Lot size: 5,000 SF minimum, 6,000 SF or more preferred. The bigger the lot, the better. The site needs to fit at least six fee-simple single-family homes, each with at least one parking space.
  • Lot shape: corner and street-to-alley lots preferred. Two street frontages make for a better site plan and access. We still consider mid-block lots.
  • Price: scales with the number of homes. For a six-home site we typically pay $800,000 – $1,500,000; for eight homes, $1,500,000 – $2,200,000; for ten homes, $2,000,000 – $3,000,000. The stronger the neighborhood and the resale value of each home, the more we can pay, so higher prices are possible in top neighborhoods.
  • Existing home: owner-occupied, or no recent renters. An owner-occupied single-family home, or a single-family home with no rental history in the last 5 years.
  • No Ellis Act filings on the property in the last 15 years.
  • Not in a high or very high fire hazard severity zone. This rules out most hillside lots.
  • Clear of the law’s other site exclusions: earthquake fault zones, special flood hazard areas and floodways, wetlands, protected habitat, hazardous waste sites and conservation easements.
  • Zoning: multifamily preferred. Multifamily-zoned lots (up to 5 acres) have the clearest path to qualify under SB 684. Single-family-zoned lots (up to 1.5 acres) qualify under SB 1123 only if the lot is vacant, which can include a lot whose only structure is abandoned and uninhabitable; they also need room for at least six homes.
  • Not already split under SB 9 or SB 684.

A screening summary, not legal advice. Final eligibility depends on the local agency’s review of the specific site.

Luxury Residential Criteria

What Makes a
Luxury Deal Work.

Our first choice is an under-improved house in a strong neighborhood that we can remodel, expand, finish to a high standard and sell in a short time frame. We also consider teardowns for new construction, but we underwrite those more conservatively because they take more capital. In the City of Los Angeles we generally price purchases so the finished home sells below the Measure ULA threshold.

  • Location: Class A, A− and B+ neighborhoods only. Westside markets preferred; hillside projects only in select cases.
  • Price: typically $1,000,000 – $3,000,000. We pay more in stronger neighborhoods where resale supports it.
  • Remodel first: keep part of the existing structure. We prefer projects where we can retain a portion of the walls or foundation so the work is permitted and financed as a remodel rather than new construction. It takes far less capital.
  • Upside: room to add square footage. Unused floor area, an expandable footprint, or a layout that can grow, so the work adds real value.
  • Teardowns and new construction: selectively. We look at ground-up luxury sites, with more conservative margin requirements given the added cost and time.
  • Hillside: exceptional views and flat building area. We pass on steep lots that need extensive retaining walls or caisson work, sites where neighboring homes look down into the property or privacy is limited, and homes of three or more levels. We rarely buy unentitled hillside land.
  • In the City of Los Angeles: exit below the Measure ULA threshold. We generally buy so the finished home sells for less than $5,400,000, avoiding the city’s “mansion tax” (threshold adjusted each July). We make an exception only when the margin and buyer depth clearly justify it.
  • Outside Measure ULA: higher purchase prices considered. In Beverly Hills proper and select Orange County and South Bay markets, where Measure ULA doesn’t apply and resale liquidity is higher than average, we’ll look at larger purchases.
  • No design review boards or Coastal Commission review. We pass on sites that need design review board approval, sit in a historic preservation overlay zone (HPOZ), or fall in the Coastal Zone.

Measure ULA threshold per the City of Los Angeles Office of Finance, effective July 1, 2026. It applies to sales within City of Los Angeles limits.

Questions

For Agents & Wholesalers.

Do you work with buyer’s and listing agents?

Yes. We work with agents on both sides and honor the agreed commission. Bring us a deal and you stay on it.

Do you buy on assignment from wholesalers?

Yes. Send deals you have under contract, with the contract price and your assignment terms, and we’ll tell you quickly whether it works for us.

How fast can you close?

When the deal calls for it, we can close quickly with cash or private financing. Timing depends on title, the seller’s needs and any due diligence the site requires; we’ll tell you our timeline with the offer.

What happens after I submit?

Within two business days you’ll get a yes, a no, or our questions. If it fits, we walk the property and make an offer.

Do you pay referral fees?

Yes. If you’re not an agent and bring us an off-market property we buy, we pay a referral fee where the law allows it. Tell us up front when you submit, and we’ll confirm terms in writing before you share the owner’s details.

Do you look at deals outside these neighborhoods?

Yes, selectively. For luxury single-family projects we also buy in Beverly Hills proper and select Orange County and South Bay markets, where Measure ULA doesn’t apply. For SB 684 and SB 1123, we also consider coastal and infill sites in the South Bay, Orange County, San Diego and Santa Barbara where new homes can achieve strong per-square-foot resale.
