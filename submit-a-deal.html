SB 684 / SB 1123 Site Criteria

What Makes an
SB 684 Site Work.

These are infill lots in the flats, usually with a dated single-family home on them. We prefer Westside markets and Class A, A− and B+ neighborhoods, and the site has to clear the tenant-history and hazard rules in the law. If a property meets most of this list, send it: we’ll run the full eligibility review.

Read the full SB 684 / 1123 guide
  • Location: infill lots in the flats. Westside markets preferred, in Class A / A− / B+ neighborhoods: Culver City, Santa Monica, Mar Vista, Marina del Rey, Venice, Brentwood and similar.
  • Existing home: owner-occupied, or no recent renters. An owner-occupied single-family home, or a single-family home with no rental history in the last 5 years.
  • No Ellis Act filings on the property in the last 15 years.
  • Not in a high or very high fire hazard severity zone. This rules out most hillside lots.
  • Clear of the law’s other site exclusions: earthquake fault zones, special flood hazard areas and floodways, wetlands, protected habitat, hazardous waste sites and conservation easements.
  • Zoning and size: a multifamily-zoned lot of up to 5 acres, or a vacant single-family-zoned lot of up to 1.5 acres. Usually 5,000 SF or larger, carrying four to ten new homes.
  • Not already split under SB 9 or SB 684.

A screening summary, not legal advice. Final eligibility depends on the local agency’s review of the specific site.

Single-Family Remodel Criteria

What Makes a
Remodel Deal Work.

For single-family homes we look for fix-and-flip remodels: an under-improved house in a strong neighborhood where we can add square footage, finish it to a high standard and sell it in a short time frame. In the City of Los Angeles we price every purchase so the finished home sells below the Measure ULA threshold; outside the city, we consider higher price points.

  • Location: Class A, A− and B+ neighborhoods only. Westside markets preferred; we consider select hillside projects.
  • Upside: room to add square footage. Unused floor area, an expandable footprint, or a layout that can grow, so the remodel adds real value.
  • Speed: short, defined scope. Remodel and expansion projects we can complete and resell quickly, rather than long ground-up builds.
  • In the City of Los Angeles: exit price below the Measure ULA threshold. We buy at a price point where the finished home sells for less than $5,400,000, so the sale doesn’t trigger the city’s “mansion tax” (threshold adjusted each July).
  • Outside Measure ULA: higher purchase prices considered. In Beverly Hills proper and select Orange County and South Bay markets, where Measure ULA doesn’t apply and resale liquidity is higher than average, we’ll look at larger purchases.
  • No design review boards or Coastal Commission review. We pass on sites that need design review board approval, sit in a historic preservation overlay zone (HPOZ), or fall in the Coastal Zone.

Measure ULA threshold per the City of Los Angeles Office of Finance, effective July 1, 2026. It applies to sales within City of Los Angeles limits.

Questions

For Agents & Wholesalers.

Do you work with buyer’s and listing agents?

Yes. We work with agents on both sides and honor the agreed commission. Bring us a deal and you stay on it.

Do you buy on assignment from wholesalers?

Yes. Send deals you have under contract, with the contract price and your assignment terms, and we’ll tell you quickly whether it works for us.

How fast can you close?

When the deal calls for it, we can close quickly with cash or private financing. Timing depends on title, the seller’s needs and any due diligence the site requires; we’ll tell you our timeline with the offer.

What happens after I submit?

Within two business days you’ll get a yes, a no, or our questions. If it fits, we walk the property and make an offer.

Do you look at deals outside these neighborhoods?

Yes, selectively. For single-family remodels we also buy in Beverly Hills proper and select Orange County and South Bay markets, where Measure ULA doesn’t apply. For SB 684 and SB 1123, we occasionally look at flat, urban land elsewhere in Los Angeles County. If you’re unsure, send it.
