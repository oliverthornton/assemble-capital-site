How a Luxury Fix-and-Flip Is Underwritten in Los Angeles

Oliver Thornton · October 24, 2026

General information only. This article is educational and is not legal, tax, financial, lending or investment advice. Do your own due diligence and confirm everything with your own qualified professionals before relying on it. Read the full disclaimer.

A luxury fix-and-flip looks simple from the outside: buy an under-improved house, renovate it beautifully, sell it for more. The difference between a good project and a painful one is decided before closing, in the underwriting. This is how experienced developers approach it in Los Angeles, in general terms.

Start With the Exit

Underwriting begins with what the finished home will sell for, its after-repair value. That number comes from recent closed sales of comparable finished homes nearby: similar size, bedrooms, lot, street quality and finish level. Listings and appreciation forecasts are not part of the base case. If the project only works because prices rise, it is a bet on the market, not a plan.

The Numbers That Matter

Purchase pricePlus closing costs and any seller credits

ConstructionHard costs for the full scope, priced by the builder
ContingencyA reserve for what the walls reveal
Carrying costsInterest, taxes, insurance and utilities for the full hold
Selling costsCommissions, transfer taxes, staging and escrow
Resale valueClosed comparable sales of finished homes

Scope Is Where Risk Hides

Older Los Angeles houses keep their secrets until the walls are open: foundations, framing, plumbing, electrical and drainage. A careful scope, inspections before closing and a realistic contingency are what keep a remodel on budget. Projects that keep part of the existing structure can often be permitted and financed as remodels; see remodel vs. new construction.

Time Is a Cost

Every month of design, permitting, construction and marketing adds interest, taxes and insurance. A schedule that slips by a few months can erase much of a project’s margin even if the sale price is right. That is why we favor short, defined scopes and price-point discipline over open-ended rebuilds.

Price-Point Discipline

In the City of Los Angeles, a finished home that sells just above the Measure ULA threshold can net less than one that sells just below it. Underwriting a luxury flip in the city now means deciding the target resale price first and fitting the scope to it.

The Downside Case

Good underwriting asks what happens if the home sells for less, takes longer to sell, or costs more to build, and whether the project still works. If a modest miss on any one of those turns the project upside down, it is too thin.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is ARV in a fix-and-flip?
After-repair value: the estimated sale price of the home once the remodel is complete, based on recent closed sales of comparable finished homes.

Why underwrite to closed sales instead of listings?
Closed sales show what buyers actually paid. Listing prices show what sellers hope to get, and can overstate value.

How much contingency should a remodel carry?
It depends on the age, condition and scope of the house, but remodels generally need more contingency than new construction because existing conditions are only fully visible once walls are opened.

Why does time matter so much on a flip?
Every month adds interest, taxes, insurance and utilities. A longer schedule can erode a project's margin even if the finished home sells at the expected price.
