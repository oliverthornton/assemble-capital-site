Real Estate Syndication for Accredited Investors in Los Angeles

Oliver Thornton · May 28, 2019

In today’s evolving real estate landscape, accredited investors are seeking smarter, more efficient ways to grow wealth without taking on the hands-on burden of owning, developing, or managing properties themselves. Real estate syndication in Los Angeles and other major markets has quickly become a preferred vehicle for those looking to gain access to institutional-quality deals, professional operators, and asymmetrical returns—all without needing to become landlords. In this guide, we’ll break down what real estate syndication is, how it works, who the key players are, and why it’s one of the most powerful wealth-building strategies for accredited investors today.

What is Real Estate Syndication?

Real estate syndication is a collaborative investment structure where multiple investors pool capital to acquire, develop, or operate a real estate asset. Typically, a syndication includes two core parties: - General Partner (GP): Also called the "sponsor," this party sources the deal, manages the project, raises capital, and oversees operations. - Limited Partners (LPs): Passive investors who contribute funds and share in profits but do not participate in daily operations. Syndications allow accredited investors to passively invest in larger-scale commercial or residential projects (e.g., multifamily, single-family portfolios, or mixed-use developments) they would not be able to acquire individually.

Why Only Accredited Investors?

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) limits participation in private placement offerings to accredited investors to reduce regulatory burdens while protecting less sophisticated individuals. An accredited investor must: - Have a net worth of over $1 million (excluding primary residence), or - Earn over $200,000 per year individually ($300,000 with spouse) This classification allows Assemble Capital and other syndicators to structure and market opportunities without registering them publicly

Key Benefits of Real Estate Syndication for Accredited Investors

  • Passive Income: No management responsibilities

  • Diversification: Spread capital across markets, asset classes, and risk levels

  • Tax Advantages: Depreciation, cost segregation, and K-1 pass-throughs

  • Access to Institutional-Grade Assets: Projects otherwise reserved for major developers

  • Professional Management: Industry experts handle acquisition, construction, leasing, and disposition

How Does Real Estate Syndication Work?

  1. Deal Sourcing: The GP identifies a viable property and negotiates terms.

  2. Underwriting: Financial modeling, due diligence, and stress testing are completed.

  3. Capital Raise: LPs are invited to invest via a private placement memorandum (PPM).

  4. Acquisition: Once funding is secured, the property is acquired.

  5. Execution: Construction, renovations, leasing, or repositioning occurs.

  6. Stabilization or Exit: Returns are distributed, and the property is refinanced or sold.

The GP-LP Relationship Explained

In most deals, GPs receive fees and a percentage of profits (typically 20-30%); LPs receive preferred returns (usually 6-10%) before profit splits. Equity is structured to align interests and reward strong performance Transparency, reporting, and communication cadence are key features of a quality GP-LP partnership.

Common Risks to Understand

  • Market Risk: Economic downturns, interest rate volatility

  • Execution Risk: Delays, budget overruns, permitting issues

  • Liquidity Risk: Capital is typically tied up for 3–7 years That said, when paired with proper due diligence, quality sponsors, and market insight, real estate syndications offer unmatched upside.

Is Syndication Right for You?

If you are an accredited investor looking for passive, tax-advantaged income with the potential for asymmetrical returns, syndication is a powerful vehicle. The key lies in aligning yourself with the right sponsor—a firm like Assemble Capital that combines deal flow, construction expertise, and investor-first transparency. Whether you're new to real estate or ready to scale your portfolio, syndication opens the door to institutional-caliber investing with personalized service.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is real estate syndication?

Real estate syndication is a structure where multiple investors pool capital, led by a general partner (sponsor) who sources, underwrites, and manages the deal, while limited partners contribute capital passively and share in the economics without day-to-day involvement.

What is a real estate syndicator?

A real estate syndicator is another term for the sponsor or general partner — the party responsible for finding the deal, raising capital from limited partners, and executing the business plan through to exit.

Who can invest in a real estate syndication?

Most real estate syndications are offered under Regulation D exemptions from SEC registration, which typically limits participation to accredited investors (and in some structures, a limited number of sophisticated non-accredited investors). Eligibility depends on the specific offering's exemption and terms.

Is real estate syndication a good investment?

Whether syndication fits an individual investor depends on their liquidity needs, risk tolerance, and time horizon — syndications are illiquid, multi-year commitments. It is not a universal answer; it is a tool that suits investors seeking passive exposure to institutional-quality real estate who understand and accept those tradeoffs.
