Remodel vs. New Construction in Los Angeles: Why Keeping the Walls Matters

Oliver Thornton · October 24, 2026

Two projects can produce nearly the same finished house and look completely different to a lender, a building department and a budget. One is a remodel that keeps part of the existing structure; the other is new construction from the ground up. In Los Angeles, that distinction often decides how much capital a project needs and how fast it moves.

How the Two Compare

Remodel / expansionNew construction
ApprovalsOften building permits onlyMay need entitlement plus permits
FinancingRenovation-style loans, usually simplerGround-up construction loans, usually more equity and controls
Capital neededGenerally lowerGenerally higher
ScheduleShorter, defined scopeLonger
Main riskHidden existing conditionsCost and schedule over a longer build

Why Keeping Part of the Structure Matters

When a project retains a meaningful portion of the existing walls or foundation, it can often be permitted and financed as a remodel rather than as new construction. That can reduce the capital a project needs and simplify the approval path. Whether a specific project qualifies depends on the city’s rules and the lender’s policies, so the decision has to be made during design, not after demolition starts.

The Trade-Off: Existing Conditions

Remodels carry a risk ground-up projects don’t: you inherit the house. Foundations, framing, plumbing and electrical only reveal their true condition once the walls are open. Disciplined remodel underwriting carries real contingency in both budget and schedule for exactly that reason.

Where Each Makes Sense

A remodel and expansion fits an under-improved house in a strong neighborhood, especially one with unused floor area or a footprint that can grow. New construction fits when the existing structure is beyond saving, or when the site supports a substantially different product, such as several fee-simple homes under SB 684. On hillside lots, both get harder: steep sites can require retaining walls and caissons that change the economics entirely.

How We Approach It

For luxury single-family projects, we prefer remodels and expansions where part of the existing structure can stay, in Class A, A− and B+ neighborhoods, with a scope we can finish and sell in a defined time frame. We consider ground-up projects selectively and underwrite them more conservatively. See our luxury residential strategy.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a remodel cheaper than new construction?
Often, but not always. A remodel that keeps part of the structure can reduce demolition, foundation and permitting scope, but hidden conditions can add cost once walls are opened.

How does financing differ for a remodel vs. new construction?
Lenders generally treat a remodel of an existing home as a lower-risk renovation loan, while ground-up construction loans tend to require more equity, more documentation and tighter draw controls.

Does a remodel need entitlement in Los Angeles?
Many remodels and additions need only building permits, not separate entitlement approvals, though hillside, historic or coastal sites can add reviews.

What can go wrong on a remodel?
Scope surprises. Existing conditions only become fully visible once walls are open, so remodels need contingency in both budget and schedule.
