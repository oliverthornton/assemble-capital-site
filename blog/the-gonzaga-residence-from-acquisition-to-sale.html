The Gonzaga Residence: A Los Angeles Remodel, From Acquisition to Market

Oliver Thornton · November 18, 2025

Every Assemble Capital project starts with the same question: does this property work at today's prices, without betting on where the market goes next? The Gonzaga Residence — a single-family home at 8404 Gonzaga Ave in Westchester — is a useful case study for how that question gets answered in practice, because unlike most of what we can share about real estate syndication in Los Angeles, this one is still in progress. The property is complete and on the market now, not sold, and this post walks through the acquisition, the design and construction strategy, and where the project actually stands today — not a financial outcome, because there isn't one to report yet.

The Opportunity: Why Westchester

The Gonzaga Residence came into the portfolio as one of seven active projects Assemble Capital is currently running, and it was the first of the seven to reach completion. Westchester isn't the neighborhood most people associate with the firm's Hollywood Hills track record, but the submarket fit the acquisition criteria for a specific reason: it sits in the path of steady owner-user demand, with proximity to Westside and LAX-area employment and a deep buyer pool at the upper end of the local market. Just as important, the housing stock in the area is old enough that a well-executed, comprehensive remodel stands out clearly against the comparable set — the same profile the firm looks for across every strategy.

The Investment Thesis: Underwrite to What's Already Sold

The acquisition was underwritten to Westchester's closed comparable sales as they stood before the property was purchased — not to a forecast of what the neighborhood might be worth by the time the project finished. That's a deliberate discipline, not a formality: if a deal only works because prices rise by a certain amount on a certain timeline, it's a bet on the market. If it already works against today's closed comps, appreciation becomes upside instead of a requirement. The Gonzaga Residence was acquired into Assemble Capital LLC with investor participation on that basis.

The Plan: A Comprehensive Remodel, Not a Rebuild

The scope was a full remodel — five bedrooms and five bathrooms across 2,780 square feet — rather than a ground-up rebuild, which meant the entitlement path was straightforward: the full remodel scope was permitted with no separate entitlement process required. That mattered for timeline discipline, since every month of holding is a month of carrying costs before any return of capital.

Construction ran under Assemble Capital's in-house construction management, consistent with the firm's vertically integrated model across acquisitions, design, permitting, construction, and eventual sale or lease. Keeping that scope in-house is less about controlling the story and more about controlling the handoffs — the points in a project's life where cost and schedule risk most often creep in.

Where the Project Stands Today

The Gonzaga Residence is complete and currently listed on the open market in the Westchester comparable set, with a sale targeted for Q3 2026. That target is not a guarantee, and it isn't a projection of what the property will ultimately sell for. Consistent with how Assemble Capital treats every active offering, deal-level economics for this project — purchase basis, total cost, and any prospective return — are shared privately with prospective investors under the applicable offering documents, not published here. If and when the sale closes, any distributed proceeds will follow the operating agreement's waterfall — the same Class A structure, an 8% preferred return paid first with sponsor capital invested in every deal, that runs across the platform.

The Takeaway

The lesson from the Gonzaga Residence so far isn't a return figure, because there isn't one yet. It's that the underwriting discipline — buy against today's comparables, keep execution in-house, plan the exit before acquisition rather than improvising it afterward — doesn't change based on whether a project has already closed. Qualified investors who want the full underwriting, current comparable set, and business plan behind this offering can request it directly. What's public here is the process, not the outcome.
