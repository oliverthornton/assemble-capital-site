What to Know Before Investing in a Private Placement

Oliver Thornton · June 25, 2025

General information only. This article is educational and is not legal, tax, financial, lending or investment advice. Do your own due diligence and confirm everything with your own qualified professionals before relying on it. Read the full disclaimer.

Private placements have become one of the most powerful tools for accredited investors seeking access to alternative assets like real estate, venture capital, or private debt — and for many, learning how to invest in a real estate syndication starts with understanding this structure. Unlike public markets or real estate crowdfunding platforms, these opportunities offer the potential for higher returns, tax advantages, and portfolio diversification — but they also require deeper understanding and due diligence.

At Assemble Capital, we view investor education as essential to long-term success. Understanding how private placements work, what risks they entail, and how to evaluate sponsors allows investors to make confident, well-informed decisions.

This article breaks down the essentials: what private placements are, how they function in real estate syndications, and what every investor should know before committing capital.

What Is a Private Placement?

A private placement is an offering of securities directly to a limited number of qualified investors — typically under Regulation D of the Securities Act of 1933. Instead of going public through an IPO, companies raise capital privately through these structured offerings.

In the context of real estate, private placements are often used to fund development or acquisition projects. Investors pool capital to purchase or improve income-producing properties, with each investor owning a portion of the equity or debt in the project.

Unlike public investments, private placements are not traded on exchanges. This allows issuers to avoid the cost and scrutiny of public filings, while giving investors access to projects with strong upside potential that aren’t available on the open market.

Key Characteristics of Private Placements:

  • Offered to accredited investors who meet income or net worth thresholds set by the SEC.

  • Usually limited to a small group of investors, often under 100 participants.

  • Governed by Private Placement Memoranda (PPMs), which disclose offering details, terms, and risks.

  • Generally illiquid, meaning investors must commit capital for a fixed period.

  • Provide direct exposure to alternative asset classes, such as real estate, infrastructure, or private credit.

In essence, a private placement is an opportunity to partner directly in high-quality investments — without the noise and volatility of the stock market.

How Private Placements Work in Real Estate

Real estate private placements follow a clear structure designed to pool investor capital and deploy it efficiently.

At Assemble Capital, this process typically unfolds through real estate syndication, where the firm acts as the sponsor — acquiring, managing, and ultimately selling properties on behalf of investors.

1. The Structure

  • Sponsor (General Partner): Oversees the deal, sources properties, secures financing, manages construction, and handles asset disposition.

  • Investors (Limited Partners): Provide capital in exchange for equity ownership and profit participation.

The sponsor’s job is to execute the business plan — from acquisition to exit — while executing the business plan; targets are objectives rather than commitments, and invested capital remains at risk.

2. The Capital Stack

Every private placement involves a structured “capital stack,” or hierarchy of funding sources:

  • Equity: Capital contributed by investors and the sponsor.

  • Debt: Senior and/or mezzanine loans from financial institutions.

  • Preferred Equity or Mezzanine Financing: Optional layers to enhance flexibility or returns.

The precise mix affects both the risk profile and the potential upside. Assemble Capital focuses on maintaining conservative leverage ratios and transparent capital structures to safeguard investor interests.

3. The Lifecycle of a Real Estate Private Placement

  1. Acquisition: Identify undervalued or high-growth properties.

  2. Capitalization: Secure funding through private offering.

  3. Value-Add Phase: Renovate, reposition, or develop the property.

  4. Stabilization: Lease-up and operational optimization.

  5. Disposition or Refinance: Exit via sale or long-term hold.

Each phase is documented, budgeted, and reported transparently to investors — ensuring visibility across the entire investment lifecycle.

The Benefits: Access, Diversification, and Control

Private placements offer distinct advantages that often outpace public investments in both structure and performance potential.

1. Access to Exclusive Opportunities

Unlike publicly traded REITs or stocks, private placements provide direct access to institutional-grade real estate projects. These are the kinds of deals typically reserved for developers, family offices, or high-net-worth investors.

With Assemble Capital, investors gain exposure to projects that combine development potential, stable cash flow, and appreciation — without the need for hands-on property management.

2. Portfolio Diversification

Real estate private placements allow investors to balance portfolios traditionally dominated by equities and bonds. Because these assets have low correlation with public markets, they provide a stabilizing counterweight during economic downturns.

A well-structured real estate placement can deliver steady distributions while preserving long-term capital value — an ideal fit for those seeking both income and protection.

3. Passive Income and Compounding Returns

Once invested, limited partners receive recurring distributions from rental income or refinancing events. Over time, this creates a compounding effect, as returns are reinvested into future offerings.

At Assemble Capital, offerings are typically structured with a preferred return and profit participation; the specific terms are set project by project in each offering's documents.

The Risks: Illiquidity and Due Diligence Challenges

As with any investment, private placements are not without risk. The key is not to eliminate risk — but to understand and manage it.

1. Illiquidity

Private placements are designed for long-term investors. Unlike public stocks, your capital is tied up for a specific duration — typically between 3 to 7 years. This illiquidity can be a disadvantage for those seeking flexibility, but it’s also what allows for higher, more stable returns.

2. Market Risk

Although well-selected assets can outperform, market downturns, construction delays, or cost overruns can affect performance. Sponsors mitigate these risks through conservative underwriting and diversified project selection.

3. Sponsor Risk

The quality of the sponsor — their experience, integrity, and operational capacity — is one of the most critical variables in private placements. Investors should always evaluate a sponsor’s track record, alignment of interest, and communication practices.

4. Information Asymmetry

Because these are private deals, investors rely heavily on the sponsor’s disclosures. Reviewing the Private Placement Memorandum (PPM) and financial projections thoroughly is essential. Assemble Capital ensures every investor has access to full data transparency, quarterly reporting, and direct communication with principals.

What to Look for in a Private Placement Sponsor

Not all sponsors are equal — and not all private placements are managed with the same rigor.

Here’s what sophisticated investors look for before committing capital:

  1. Track Record of Execution: Past performance across multiple market cycles.

  2. Alignment of Interest: A sponsor with real exposure, such as personal guarantees on the project debt.

  3. Transparency: Clear communication, regular reporting, and accessibility.

  4. Operational Expertise: Proven ability to handle acquisition, development, and asset management.

  5. Legal & Structural Integrity: Proper entity formation, compliant offering documents, and defined exit strategies.

At Assemble Capital, these aren’t optional — they’re foundational. Every deal is structured with a clear investment thesis, conservative assumptions, and third-party verification where applicable.

How Assemble Capital Structures Investor Protection

At the core of Assemble Capital’s strategy is capital preservation before profit.

1. Aligned Incentives

Assemble’s principals personally guarantee the loans on every project, so the debt risk sits with the sponsor rather than with investors, who never sign personal guarantees on project financing. No performance fees are earned until preferred returns are met.

2. Conservative Leverage

Each project’s debt exposure is modeled to withstand market fluctuations. By maintaining healthy debt service coverage ratios and low loan-to-value ratios, Assemble reduces downside risk without limiting upside potential.

3. Transparent Reporting

Investors receive quarterly financial statements, progress photos, and project summaries — offering total visibility into the use and performance of their capital.

4. Legal Compliance and Due Diligence

Every offering is structured under SEC Regulation D, with accredited-investor verification handled according to the specific exemption each offering relies on. Assemble works with experienced securities attorneys and CPA firms to maintain compliance and accountability.

5. Horizontally Integrated Operations

Because Assemble Capital controls the full lifecycle — from acquisition and construction to sale — there are fewer intermediaries, fewer markups, and tighter oversight of every dollar invested.

This structure minimizes risk leakage and enhances investor confidence.

Private placements can be a powerful vehicle for wealth creation — but only when guided by education, transparency, and partnership.

The most successful investors understand that returns follow structure. When deals are built with integrity, data, and discipline, private investments can outperform traditional markets while delivering real, tangible value.

At Assemble Capital, every investor is treated like a partner — not a participant. Our approach is simple: clarity, communication, and control.

Because investing isn’t just about chasing yield — it’s about building something that lasts.
