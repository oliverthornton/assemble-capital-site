SB 684 in Santa Monica: Small-Lot Homes in a Supply-Constrained City

Oliver Thornton · October 24, 2026

Few cities in Southern California need new starter homes more than Santa Monica, and few have been harder to build in. California’s SB 684 changes that for a specific kind of site: a multifamily-zoned lot in the flats that can be subdivided into several small fee-simple homes, approved ministerially with no public hearing.

Why Santa Monica Is Different

Santa Monica combines strong demand, limited new supply and a large share of rent-controlled housing. That last point matters most for SB 684: the law protects existing renters, and Santa Monica’s rent-control history means tenant history can disqualify many otherwise attractive lots. The sites that work are typically owner-occupied homes, or properties that have not been rented in years, on multifamily-zoned land.

A Recent Example: 1949 17th Street

At 1949 17th Street, a principal-owned project developed through Thornton Development Group, the city approved nine fee-simple homes on one subdivided lot under SB 684. It was only the second SB 684 approval in Santa Monica, and it took about eight months from the start of entitlement to City approval. The property had not been used as rental housing in more than 30 years, which kept it clear of the rent-control issues that stop many sites. Eight of the planned homes are about 1,150 square feet and one is about 1,600, each on its own parcel with its own rooftop deck. The project is now near ready-to-issue permits, with construction expected to start in November 2026 (estimated).

What Makes a Santa Monica Lot Work

  • Zoning. Multifamily zoning gives the clearest path; single-family lots qualify under SB 1123 only if vacant.
  • Tenant history. Owner-occupied, or no rental history in the last five years, and no Ellis Act withdrawal in the last fifteen. See Ellis Act and tenant history.
  • Size. Enough land for at least six homes; most strong sites are 6,000 square feet or more.
  • Hazards. Outside fire hazard severity zones and the law’s other exclusions.
  • Coastal review. Sites that need Coastal Commission review add time and uncertainty; we generally pass on them.

Taxes on Sale

Santa Monica is outside the City of Los Angeles, so Measure ULA does not apply. The city has its own transfer tax on higher-value sales, which sellers should factor into pricing. Entry-level fee-simple homes generally sell well below the price points where the steepest transfer taxes apply.

Thinking About Selling?

If you own a multifamily-zoned lot in Santa Monica with an older home on it, the land may be worth more to a small-lot developer than the house is to a typical buyer. Read selling your lot to an SB 684 developer, or send us the address for a quick read.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does SB 684 apply in Santa Monica?
Yes. SB 684 is state law and applies in Santa Monica to qualifying multifamily-zoned lots, with SB 1123 extending it to qualifying vacant single-family lots. The city processes applications under its own procedures.

How does Santa Monica rent control affect SB 684 sites?
Tenant history is one of the first screens. Sites with recent tenants or rent-controlled units can be disqualified or face additional requirements, so owner-occupied homes, or properties with no rental history, are the strongest candidates.

How long does SB 684 approval take in Santa Monica?
On 1949 17th Street, a principal-owned project, it took about eight months from the start of entitlement to City approval. Every site differs.

Does Measure ULA apply in Santa Monica?
No. Measure ULA applies only within City of Los Angeles limits. Santa Monica has its own transfer tax on higher-value sales.
